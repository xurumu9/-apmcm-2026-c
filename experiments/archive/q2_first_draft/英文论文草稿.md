# Draft: Signal degradation and scenario-dependent communication range

**Working draft for team review; citations, thresholds, assumptions and integration must be checked against the contest rules and the final team model.**

## Data and fidelity measure

We used the supplied correlations for three African penguin sea-surface call types (flat, modulated and two-voice). For each call type, spectrogram, amplitude-spectrum and amplitude-envelope correlations compared a recording at 1 m with recordings at 4, 8, 16, 32, 64, 128 and 256 m. We calculated the mean of the available correlations at each distance, treating missing entries as missing rather than zero. The number of available correlations per distance ranged from 190 to 300.

For call type c at distance d, denote the three mean correlations by S_c(d), A_c(d), and E_c(d), respectively. The baseline composite fidelity is F_c(d) = [S_c(d) + A_c(d) + E_c(d)] / 3. Equal weights are a transparent starting assumption, not established perceptual weights; the three components may also overlap in their acoustic information. We interpolate F_c linearly between adjacent measured distances and do not extrapolate beyond 4–256 m. The measure describes acoustic similarity, not individual hearing or behavioral response probability.

## Effective range and sensitivity

For a threshold T, define the continuous range as the first distance, moving outward from 4 m, at which the interpolated fidelity falls below T. Thresholds 0.5, 0.6, and 0.7 represent illustrative recognition stringency levels rather than validated biological cutoffs. Under equal weights and linear-distance interpolation, first-crossing ranges at T = 0.5 were 89.90, 150.47, and 180.10 m for flat, modulated, and two-voice calls, respectively. Corresponding ranges at T = 0.7 were 36.64, 84.04, and 15.83 m. The two-voice result at T = 0.7 is highly sensitive to a non-monotone segment: the curve crosses down at approximately 15.83 m, up at 18.44 m, and down again at 97.93 m. It should therefore not be interpreted as a robust ranking of long-range transmission.

We repeated the calculation using four weight settings (equal weights and each of the three 0.6/0.2/0.2 permutations) and two interpolation scales (distance and log distance). Across these eight specified scenarios, the T = 0.5 ranges were 62.45–101.04 m, 119.05–166.79 m, and 147.59–194.47 m for flat, modulated, and two-voice calls, respectively. At T = 0.7 the corresponding ranges were 22.70–51.66 m, 23.04–97.25 m, and 9.85–128.33 m. These are model-scenario ranges, not confidence intervals. The overlap and the sensitivity of the first-crossing definition preclude a universal ordering independent of modeling assumptions.

As a limited internal check, each of the five interior distance levels was omitted in turn and predicted from the two neighboring retained levels. Mean absolute errors for linear-distance versus log-distance interpolation were 0.0474 versus 0.0417 for flat, 0.0521 versus 0.0558 for modulated, and 0.0510 versus 0.0698 for two-voice. Only five points per call type were assessed; this is not an independent validation of field generalization.

## Relationship to behavioral playback and limitations

The playback dataset contains 139 experiments with one call playback and one control record per experiment ID. Descriptive response rates by call type and distance band are reported separately. In 90 pairs, the recorded distance differed between call and control, so the paired difference should not be treated as a distance-controlled causal effect. Behavioral response is also not equivalent to sound detection. The fidelity measure and behavioral model must consequently be interpreted separately and combined only after the response model and communication index are specified.

The original propagation study used three call examples per type, each repeated ten times; pairwise comparisons can yield up to 300 correlations per type and distance. These correlations share recordings and cannot be treated as 300 independent call examples [1]. The supplied tables do not establish all pairing and clustering labels required for valid cluster-level inference. We therefore do not report the naive independent-row bootstrap as a confidence interval; the scenario ranges above reflect selected modeling choices only. In addition, the propagation experiment was conducted over an inland reservoir under windless conditions, so application to rough or noisy marine habitats is an extrapolation [1].

An illustrative adverse-condition scenario reduces the composite fidelity at every distance by 20%, without claiming that this represents a measured noise level. At T = 0.5 the first-crossing distances then become 56.37, 106.50, and 137.77 m for flat, modulated, and two-voice calls, respectively. This scenario does not estimate effects of a specific vessel or sound-pressure level.
